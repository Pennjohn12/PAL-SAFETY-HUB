## Chapter 1 — PAL Expectations, Daily Planning, Training, PPE, and Fall Protection

### Scene 1 — Welcome to PAL

**Narration**

Welcome to PAL Environmental Services employee orientation. Before working on a PAL project, every employee must understand PAL’s basic safety expectations, required training, jobsite rules, emergency procedures, and reporting responsibilities.

This orientation is an introduction. It does not authorize you to perform work for which you have not been trained, and it does not replace site-specific orientation or task-specific instruction. If you do not understand a rule or do not have the proper training, equipment, or controls, stop and ask your foreman, supervisor, general superintendent, or PAL Environmental Health and Safety representative before continuing.

### Scene 2 — Orientation Goals, Pre-Task Planning, and Stretch and Flex

**Narration**

PAL’s safety program is built around protecting employees, preventing incidents through planning and risk assessment, educating the workforce, meeting or exceeding applicable requirements, and maintaining clear communication.

Before work begins each day, the foreman or supervisor conducts a brief safety meeting and reviews the pre-task plan with the crew. Discuss the day’s activities, identify hazards, confirm required controls and personal protective equipment, and make sure each employee understands the assignment. If the work, crew, equipment, or site conditions change, stop and update the plan before continuing.

PAL employees also participate in Stretch and Flex before beginning work each day. Controlled movements help warm up the body, reduce stiffness, and prepare employees for the physical demands of fireproofing, demolition, and material handling. Follow the instructor, remain within a comfortable range of motion, and report any limitation or concern before starting work.

Every employee is expected to speak up, ask questions, and use stop-work authority when a task cannot be performed safely.

**Visual direction**

- Foreman leads a morning safety meeting around a pre-task plan.
- Crew identifies overhead, access, dust, fall, and material-handling hazards.
- Workers perform controlled shoulder, arm, back, and leg warm-up movements.
- Show the plan being updated after conditions change.

### Scene 3 — Keep Training Records Current

**Narration**

Before working on a PAL project, employees must possess the training records required for their assigned work. These may include New York City Site Safety Training or OSHA training, fall-prevention training, scaffold-user training, equipment-operator qualifications, and respiratory fit testing and medical clearance when respiratory protection is required.

Required records must remain current throughout your PAL employment. Know when your cards, certifications, medical clearances, and fit tests require renewal. Do not wait for a credential to expire. Provide updated records to PAL before the expiration date, and never perform work that requires a credential that is missing, expired, or awaiting renewal.

Respirator use requires the proper medical clearance, fit test, training, and authorization. During fit testing, place the respirator correctly over the nose and chin, adjust the straps, and follow the qualified fit-test administrator’s instructions. Facial hair or anything else that interferes with the sealing surface is not permitted.

**Visual direction**

- PAL safety staff reviews SST/OSHA, scaffold, fall-protection, equipment, medical-clearance, and fit-test records.
- Show expiration dates and a renewal reminder before expiration.
- Replace treadmill footage with a worker correctly donning a respirator under qualified supervision.
- Show strap adjustment, facial seal, and the required seal check.

### Scene 4 — Required PPE

**Narration**

Report to the job prepared with the required personal protective equipment. PAL’s minimum jobsite PPE includes an approved hard hat, high-visibility vest, approved safety glasses, work boots, long pants, proper shirt coverage, and task-appropriate gloves.

Inspect PPE before use and replace damaged or defective equipment. As a worker enters the site, confirm each required item is present and worn correctly.

Additional task-specific PPE may include a face shield, hearing protection, protective coveralls, rubber gloves or boots, and respiratory protection. A face shield does not replace safety glasses. A respirator may only be worn after the employee has completed all required medical, fit-test, training, and authorization steps. PPE must match the hazard and the approved work plan.

**Visual direction**

- Keep the worker-entering-the-jobsite sequence.
- Use deliberate close-ups of hard hat, safety glasses, vest, gloves, boots, pants, and shirt coverage.
- Show face shield, hearing protection, coveralls, and respirator separately as task-specific PPE.
- Use PAL-relevant fireproofing and demolition surroundings.

### Scene 5 — Fall Protection

**Narration**

Fall protection is one of PAL’s highest safety priorities. Before wearing a harness, inspect the webbing, stitching, buckles, D-rings, lanyard, connectors, and inspection label. Put the harness on correctly, remove twists, secure every buckle, and adjust the straps for a proper fit.

PAL requires a harness at all times when operating or working from a scissor lift. Only trained and authorized employees may operate the lift. Complete the required pre-use inspection and connect the required lanyard or restraint system only to the manufacturer-designated anchor point inside the platform. Never connect to the guardrail. Keep both feet on the platform floor. Never stand, sit, or climb on the guardrails, use a ladder or box inside the lift, or extend outside the platform. Lower and reposition the lift when the work cannot be reached safely.

When working from a boom lift, connect to the manufacturer-designated anchor point using the required fall-protection system. When working near an exposed edge, use the approved guardrail, lifeline, restraint, or fall-arrest system established for the work area.

Beam clamps, column straps, and other anchorage devices may only be used when approved, properly rated, correctly installed, and authorized for the application. Never tie off to piping, conduit, scaffold guardrails, or an improvised anchor.

Protect workers below. Establish an exclusion zone using danger tape, rigid barricades, or another site-approved system. Do not allow anyone to walk or work beneath overhead operations.

**Visual direction**

- Detailed harness inspection and correct fitting.
- Scissor-lift pre-use inspection, closed gate, harness, and tie-off to labeled anchor point.
- Boom-lift connection to designated anchor.
- Approved beam clamp, column strap, and lifeline near an exposed edge.
- Danger tape and rigid barricades blocking access below.
- Never show tying off to a guardrail or improvised point as acceptable.

## Chapter 2 — Electrical Safety, Ladders Last, Tools, LOTO, and Material Handling

### Scene 6 — Electrical Safety

**Narration**

Inspect extension cords, plugs, temporary wiring, and ground-fault circuit interrupters before use. Cords must be heavy-duty, grounded, undamaged, and protected by the required GFCI. Test the GFCI before each shift. Never alter plug prongs or use a cord with exposed conductors, damaged insulation, a broken plug, or an improper taped repair. Tag damaged equipment, remove it from service, and report it immediately.

Cords lying across floors create trip hazards and can be damaged by workers, carts, debris, or equipment. Route cords away from walkways whenever possible. When cords must be elevated, use approved temporary cord hangers or supports and maintain safe overhead clearance. Never hang cords from sprinkler piping, sharp edges, or surfaces that could damage the insulation.

**Visual direction**

- Cord creating a clear floor-level trip hazard.
- Crew reroutes it using approved overhead cord supports—not piping.
- Close-ups of exposed conductors, damaged plugs, and missing ground prongs.
- Damaged cord tagged “DO NOT USE” and removed.

### Scene 7 — PAL Ladders Last Policy

**Narration**

PAL follows a Ladders Last Policy because ladder falls can cause serious or fatal injuries. During pre-task planning, select the safest available work platform. The required order is: first, an aerial lift; second, scaffolding; third, a podium or platform ladder; and fourth, a traditional portable ladder only as the last permitted option.

A traditional stepladder or extension ladder may only be used when a safer work platform is not practical and the ladder is permitted for the task. Inspect every ladder before use. Place it on a firm, level surface. Face the ladder and maintain three points of contact while climbing. Never stand on the top step, straddle the top, overreach, or move a ladder while someone is on it.

Podium and platform ladders are preferred because they provide a guarded standing platform. Extension ladders must be positioned at the proper angle, secured against movement, and extend the required distance above the landing when used for access.

If an approved PAL work plan requires fall protection while using an extension ladder, connect only to a properly rated and authorized anchorage system, such as an approved beam clamp, column strap, or lifeline installed for that purpose. Never create an improvised tie-off.

**Visual direction**

- Clearly show the four-step selection hierarchy.
- Use accurate footage of aerial lift, scaffold, podium/platform ladder, then traditional ladder.
- Show an extension ladder secured correctly.
- Show only approved anchorage under an authorized work plan.

### Scene 8 — Tools, LOTO, and Material Handling

**Narration**

Only trained employees may operate hand and power tools. Inspect the tool, guard, cord, handle, and accessory before every use. Use the correct accessory and required PPE. Maintain stable footing, secure the work, and disconnect power before changing or adjusting an attachment. Remove any damaged tool or missing guard from service.

Hazardous energy may be electrical, hydraulic, pneumatic, steam, spring, or another stored source. Work may begin only after authorized personnel have properly de-energized, isolated, locked, tagged, and verified the equipment. Only the person who installed a personal lock may remove it, except under an approved company procedure.

Plan every lift. Consider the load’s weight, shape, balance, travel path, and final placement. Keep the load close, lift with your legs, and avoid twisting. If an item is too heavy, long, or awkward for one person, stop and get additional crew members.

When manual handling is not appropriate, use an electric pallet jack, forklift, hoist, cart, dolly, or other approved equipment operated by trained and authorized personnel. Inspect lifting equipment and rigging. Never exceed capacity, place your hands in a pinch point, or stand beneath a suspended load.

**Visual direction**

- Tool inspection, guard check, and damaged tool removal.
- Correct lockout/tagout with energy verification.
- Worker declines to lift an oversized item alone.
- Team lift, electric pallet jack, hoist, and authorized forklift operator.
- Do not repeat the tool-inspection narration as occurred in version one.

## Chapter 3 — Fire, Housekeeping, HazCom, Scaffolds, and Lifts

### Scene 9 — Hot Work, Temporary Heat, and Housekeeping

**Narration**

Hot-work controls are required when cutting steel, performing torch demolition, or completing another operation that creates flames, sparks, or heat. Obtain the required permit before work begins. Inspect the area, remove or protect combustible materials, and establish the required fire watch. Keep the correct fire extinguisher immediately accessible. The fire watch must understand the assignment, maintain an unobstructed view, and remain in place for the period required by the permit and project rules.

Fireproofing operations may require temporary heated enclosures. Only approved heaters may be used under PAL’s procedure and the manufacturer’s instructions. Maintain required clearances, control fuel sources, provide ventilation, and use required atmospheric or carbon-monoxide monitoring. Tarped work areas must have clearly marked entrances and exits. Never block emergency egress.

Good housekeeping is required throughout the shift. Keep walking paths, stairs, doors, and fire equipment clear. Scrape accumulated fireproofing material from floors and promptly remove overspray, scrap, and demolition debris. Address spills, slippery surfaces, cords, and trip hazards before someone is injured.

**Visual direction**

- Torch cutting with approved hot-work permit and fire watch.
- Proper extinguisher placement.
- Approved torpedo-style heater with safe clearance, ventilation, and monitoring.
- Tarped enclosure with clearly visible entrance and exit signs.
- Crew scrapes overspray and removes demolition debris while preserving a clear path.

### Scene 10 — Hazard Communication and Material Storage

**Narration**

Know where Safety Data Sheets are maintained and review the SDS before using an unfamiliar product. Every original and secondary container must be properly labeled. Never use an unknown or unlabeled material.

Store hazardous materials in the approved designated area, cabinet, or storage room—not loose on the work floor. Follow the SDS, project rules, fire-code requirements, and PAL procedures for separation, ventilation, secondary containment, and incompatible materials. Report a leaking, damaged, or improperly stored container immediately.

**Visual direction**

- Worker locates and reviews an SDS.
- Proper GHS labels and labeled secondary containers.
- Approved storage cabinet, designated storage room, and secondary containment.
- Contrast correct storage with an unacceptable container left on the floor.

### Scene 11 — Rolling and Baker Scaffolds

**Narration**

Scaffolds may only be erected, modified, or dismantled by trained and authorized personnel under the direction of a competent person. Inspect the scaffold before use each day and after any event that could affect its condition. Complete and sign the required inspection documentation.

Rolling and baker scaffolds must have the required platform, access, guardrails, and locking casters. Lock the wheels before use. Maintain a stable base and follow the manufacturer’s requirements. Never move a mobile scaffold while someone is on it, climb the cross braces, or stand on the guardrails.

**Visual direction**

- Replace the previous scaffold footage.
- Show a realistic rolling or baker scaffold being assembled and inspected.
- Close-up of locking casters, platform, access, guardrails, and signed daily inspection.

### Scene 12 — Scissor Lifts and Boom Lifts

**Narration**

Only properly trained and authorized employees may operate a scissor lift or boom lift. Before every use, inspect and document the controls, emergency lowering system, guardrails, entry gate, tires, alarms, and designated anchor points. Remove defective equipment from service.

PAL requires the operator and every employee in a scissor lift to wear the required harness and connect to the manufacturer-designated safety point inside the platform. Keep the entry gate closed and both feet on the platform floor. Never climb or stand on the guardrails, place a ladder or box inside the lift, or extend outside the platform. Lower and reposition the lift instead of overreaching.

Follow the approved fall-protection requirements for boom lifts and connect only to designated anchor points. Maintain awareness above, below, and on every side of the lift.

**Visual direction**

- Certified operator and documented pre-use inspection.
- Correct harness fitting and tie-off inside scissor lift.
- Gate closed, feet on floor, no overreaching.
- Boom lift with correct anchor-point connection and overhead awareness.

## Chapter 4 — Heavy Equipment, Confined Spaces, and Environmental Hazards

### Scene 13 — Heavy Equipment

**Narration**

Heavy equipment may only be operated by trained, qualified, and authorized employees. Complete the required inspection, wear the seat belt, and maintain three points of contact while entering and exiting equipment.

Workers on foot must remain outside blind spots, swing radiuses, and loading areas. Listen for alarms and make eye contact with the operator before approaching or crossing the equipment’s path. Operators must watch for overhead power lines and underground utilities. Never walk beside or stand near equipment while it is being loaded.

**Visual direction**

- Authorized operator performs inspection.
- Exclusion zone around excavator or loader.
- Worker makes eye contact before approaching.
- Show overhead-line and underground-utility awareness.

### Scene 14 — Confined Spaces

**Narration**

Confined-space entry requires specialized training, authorization, and an approved entry procedure. Potential hazards include oxygen deficiency or enrichment, flammable atmosphere, toxic gases, entrapment, and engulfment. Never enter because a space looks or smells safe.

Maintain safe and unobstructed access for entry and emergency exit. Test the atmosphere using a properly calibrated meter for the hazards identified by the entry plan. This commonly includes oxygen level, flammable atmosphere, and applicable toxic gases. Provide ventilation when required and continue monitoring as directed.

Required personnel and equipment—including the attendant, communication method, and rescue provisions—must be in place before entry.

**Visual direction**

- Calibrated multi-gas meter, forced-air ventilation duct, attendant, communication, and clear access.
- Worker stops at confined-space warning until authorization is confirmed.

### Scene 15 — Asbestos, Lead, Silica, Dust Control, and Waste

**Narration**

Only trained and authorized personnel may disturb or remove asbestos-containing material or lead. Follow the approved work plan and all applicable engineering controls, regulated-area requirements, PPE, hygiene, air-monitoring, and waste procedures.

Respirable crystalline silica can cause serious illness. Exposure may occur during cutting, grinding, drilling, chipping, and demolition. Establish and mark the controlled work area before starting. Keep unauthorized employees and the public outside the area.

Control dust at its source using required wet methods, HEPA-filtered tools, HEPA vacuums, dust collection, and properly sized negative-air machines. Inspect equipment and filters, maintain containment, and follow required decontamination procedures. Never use uncontrolled dry sweeping or compressed air to clean hazardous dust.

Wear the respirator, filters, protective clothing, and other PPE specified by the work plan. Package, label, store, and dispose of regulated waste correctly. Never mix hazardous or regulated waste with ordinary construction debris.

**Visual direction**

- Containment, regulated-area signs, and restricted access.
- Correct respirator and filter inspection.
- Wet methods, HEPA tools, HEPA vacuum, and negative-air machine.
- Controlled demolition dust, decontamination, and labeled waste containers.

## Chapter 5 — Emergency Response, Reporting, Workplace Rules, and Closing

### Scene 16 — Emergency Action Plan

**Narration**

Every project must post a site-specific Emergency Action Plan. Before beginning work, know the alarms, emergency contacts, evacuation route, and muster point. Keep entrances, exits, stairs, and emergency routes unobstructed.

During an emergency, remain calm and walk—do not run. Shut down equipment only when it is safe to do so. Report to the muster point and remain there until you are accounted for and released. Notify the proper emergency authorities first, followed by PAL supervision and EH&S. Do not speak to reporters or post unapproved information about an incident.

**Visual direction**

- Workers calmly evacuate to a clearly marked muster point.
- Supervisor takes attendance.
- Emergency routes remain visible and clear.

### Scene 17 — Immediate Injury and Incident Reporting

**Narration**

Report every injury, illness, exposure, property-damage incident, environmental release, and near miss to your foreman or supervisor immediately—no matter how small it appears. Do not wait until the next day, the following week, or until the condition becomes worse.

Immediate reporting allows PAL to provide appropriate care, document what occurred, correct hazards, and prevent another employee from being injured. Reporting is required. Hiding or delaying an injury can prevent the incident from being handled correctly.

If an employee is removed from work or receives medical restrictions, provide the required documentation to PAL. Before returning, submit the appropriate doctor’s note or return-to-work release stating whether you may resume full duty or require approved restrictions. Light duty may be available when authorized and approved.

No employee will be penalized for refusing work they reasonably believe is unsafe. Stop, move to a safe location, and notify supervision.

**Visual direction**

- Worker immediately notifies foreman about a minor injury.
- Foreman arranges care and completes same-day documentation.
- Doctor’s release and work restrictions reviewed before reassignment.
- Employee uses stop-work authority appropriately.

### Scene 18 — Drugs, Alcohol, Smoking, and Testing

**Narration**

PAL maintains a drug-free and alcohol-free workplace. Alcohol use is prohibited during the workday, including lunch. Illegal drugs, controlled substances, drug paraphernalia, and reporting to work impaired are prohibited. Smoking is not permitted on New York City construction sites.

Employees and applicants must comply with PAL’s testing policy, which may include pre-assignment, random, post-accident, reasonable-suspicion, return-to-duty, and follow-up testing.

**Visual direction**

- Professional policy visuals; avoid dramatized drug use.
- No-smoking and drug-free workplace notices.

### Scene 19 — Closing Summary

**Narration**

Safety at PAL depends on planning, training, communication, and personal responsibility. Participate in the daily safety meeting and Stretch and Flex. Keep your training records current. Wear and inspect the required PPE. Follow PAL’s Ladders Last Policy. Use fall protection and approved anchor points. Maintain barricades and protect people below.

Control electrical, fire, dust, and material-handling hazards. Keep the work area clean. Operate equipment only when trained and authorized. Report every injury, incident, exposure, and near miss immediately.

If you are not trained, authorized, equipped, or certain that the work can be completed safely, stop and ask before continuing. Thank you for completing PAL Environmental Services orientation.
