## Chapter 1 — PAL Expectations, Training, PPE, and Fall Protection

### 1. Welcome to PAL

Welcome to PAL Environmental Services employee orientation. Before working on a PAL project, every employee must understand PAL’s safety expectations, required training, jobsite rules, emergency procedures, and reporting responsibilities.

This orientation does not authorize untrained work or replace site-specific instruction. If you are not trained, equipped, authorized, or certain, stop and ask supervision before continuing.

### 2. Daily Planning and Stretch and Flex

Before work each day, the foreman or supervisor conducts a safety meeting and reviews the pre-task plan. Discuss the work, identify hazards, confirm controls and PPE, and make sure everyone understands the assignment. If conditions change, stop and update the plan.

PAL employees also participate in daily Stretch and Flex. Controlled movements warm up the body and prepare employees for fireproofing, demolition, and material handling. Stay within a comfortable range and report any limitation.

### 3. Keep Training Current

Employees must maintain the records required for their work, including applicable safety, fall-prevention, scaffold, equipment-operator, and respiratory qualifications.

Know every renewal date and submit updated records before expiration. Never perform work requiring a credential that is missing, expired, or awaiting renewal.

Respirators require medical clearance, fit testing, training, and authorization. Don and adjust the respirator correctly, complete the seal check, and ensure nothing interferes with the facial seal.

### 4. Required PPE

PAL’s minimum jobsite PPE includes an approved hard hat, high-visibility vest, safety glasses, work boots, long pants, proper shirt coverage, and task-appropriate gloves. Inspect every item and replace damaged equipment.

Tasks may also require a face shield, hearing protection, coveralls, rubber gloves or boots, and respiratory protection. A face shield does not replace safety glasses. Task-specific PPE must match the hazard and approved work plan.

### 5. Fall Protection

Fall protection is one of PAL’s highest priorities. Inspect your harness, including the webbing, stitching, buckles, D-rings, lanyard, connectors, and inspection label. Put it on without twists, secure every buckle, and adjust it for a proper fit.

PAL requires a harness in scissor lifts. Trained and authorized operators must complete the pre-use inspection, close the gate, and connect only to the designated point inside the platform. Never tie to a guardrail, climb the rails, use a ladder or box inside the lift, or extend outside. Reposition the lift instead of overreaching.

In a boom lift, use the designated anchor. Near an exposed edge, use the approved guardrail, lifeline, restraint, or fall-arrest system. Beam clamps, column straps, and other anchors must be rated, installed, and authorized. Never improvise.

Protect people below. Establish an exclusion zone with danger tape, rigid barricades, or another approved system. Do not allow anyone beneath overhead work.

### Checkpoint 1

When may an employee use a respirator? After receiving the required medical clearance, fit test, training, and authorization.

## Chapter 2 — Electrical Safety, Ladders Last, Tools, and Material Handling

### 6. Electrical Safety

Inspect cords, plugs, temporary wiring, and GFCIs before use. Cords must be heavy-duty, grounded, undamaged, and protected by the required GFCI. Test the GFCI each shift. Tag damaged equipment, remove it from service, and report it.

Cords across floors create trip hazards and can be damaged by traffic or equipment. Route them away from walkways. When elevation is required, use approved cord hangers and maintain overhead clearance. Never hang cords from sprinkler piping, sharp edges, or anything that may damage the insulation.

### 7. PAL Ladders Last

PAL follows a Ladders Last Policy because ladder falls can cause serious or fatal injuries. Select access equipment in this order: first, an aerial lift; second, scaffolding; third, a podium or platform ladder; and fourth, a traditional portable ladder only as the last permitted option.

Use a traditional ladder only when a safer platform is not practical and the ladder is permitted. Inspect and stabilize it, face it, and maintain three points of contact while climbing. Never use the top step, overreach, or move an occupied ladder.

Podium ladders provide a guarded platform. Secure extension ladders at the proper angle and height. When an approved plan requires fall protection, connect only to a rated and authorized anchor or lifeline. Never improvise.

### 8. Tools, LOTO, and Material Handling

Only trained employees may operate tools. Inspect guards, cords, handles, and accessories. Use correct PPE, secure the work, disconnect power before adjustments, and remove damaged tools from service.

Authorized personnel must de-energize, isolate, lock, tag, and verify hazardous energy before work begins. Personal locks may only be removed under the approved procedure.

Plan every lift. Keep the load close, lift with your legs, and avoid twisting. Get help with heavy or awkward material. When manual handling is unsafe, use approved equipment operated by trained personnel. Never exceed capacity, enter a pinch point, or stand beneath a suspended load.

### Checkpoint 2

What is PAL’s Ladders Last order? Aerial lift, scaffolding, podium or platform ladder, and a traditional portable ladder only as the last permitted option.

## Chapter 3 — Fire, Housekeeping, HazCom, Scaffolds, and Lifts

### 9. Hot Work, Temporary Heat, and Housekeeping

Hot-work controls apply to torch demolition, steel cutting, and other flame- or spark-producing work. Obtain the permit, protect combustibles, establish the fire watch, and keep the correct extinguisher accessible. The fire watch remains as required by the permit and project rules.

Temporary fireproofing enclosures may use approved heaters under PAL procedures. Maintain clearances, ventilation, fuel controls, and required monitoring. Mark entrances and exits and never block egress.

Maintain housekeeping throughout the shift. Keep paths, stairs, doors, and fire equipment clear. Scrape accumulated fireproofing and remove overspray, scrap, and demolition debris. Correct slip and trip hazards immediately.

### 10. Hazard Communication and Storage

Know where Safety Data Sheets are maintained and review the SDS before using an unfamiliar product. Label every original and secondary container. Never use an unknown or unlabeled material.

Store hazardous materials in the approved area, cabinet, or storage room—not loose on the work floor. Follow the SDS, project rules, fire-code requirements, and PAL procedures for separation, ventilation, secondary containment, and incompatible materials.

### 11. Rolling and Baker Scaffolds

Only trained and authorized personnel under a competent person may erect, modify, or dismantle scaffolds. Inspect scaffolds daily and after anything that may affect them. Complete and sign the required inspection.

Rolling and baker scaffolds require the proper platform, access, guardrails, and locking casters. Lock the wheels before use. Never move a scaffold while occupied, climb cross braces, or stand on guardrails.

### 12. Scissor and Boom Lifts

Only trained and authorized employees may operate lifts. Before use, inspect and document the controls, emergency lowering system, guardrails, gate, tires, alarms, and anchor points. Remove defective equipment from service.

PAL requires harnesses in scissor lifts. Connect only to the designated point inside the platform, close the gate, and keep both feet on the floor. Never climb the rails or lean outside. Follow required boom-lift fall protection and remain aware above, below, and around the equipment.

### Checkpoint 3

What should you do with an unknown or unlabeled container? Do not use it. Isolate it if safe and notify supervision.

## Chapter 4 — Heavy Equipment, Confined Spaces, and Environmental Hazards

### 13. Heavy Equipment

Only trained, qualified, and authorized employees may operate heavy equipment. Complete the inspection, wear the seat belt, and use three points of contact.

Workers must remain outside blind spots, swing radiuses, and loading areas. Listen for alarms and make eye contact before approaching. Operators must watch for overhead lines and underground utilities. Never stand near equipment being loaded.

### 14. Confined Spaces

Confined-space entry requires specialized training, authorization, and an approved procedure. Hazards may include unsafe oxygen levels, flammable atmospheres, toxic gases, entrapment, and engulfment. Never enter because a space looks or smells safe.

Maintain clear entry and emergency access. Use a calibrated meter for the hazards identified by the plan, provide ventilation, and continue monitoring as required. The attendant, communication system, entry equipment, and rescue provisions must be ready before entry.

### 15. Asbestos, Lead, Silica, Dust, and Waste

Only trained and authorized personnel may disturb asbestos or lead. Follow the approved work plan, controls, regulated-area requirements, PPE, monitoring, and waste procedures.

Silica exposure may occur during cutting, grinding, drilling, chipping, and demolition. Mark the controlled area and keep unauthorized people out. Control dust with required wet methods, HEPA tools and vacuums, dust collection, and negative-air machines. Maintain containment and never use uncontrolled dry sweeping or compressed air on hazardous dust.

Wear the respirator, filters, protective clothing, and other PPE required by the plan. Package, label, store, and dispose of regulated waste correctly. Never mix it with ordinary debris.

### Checkpoint 4

What is required before confined-space entry? Training, authorization, an approved procedure, atmospheric testing, required equipment and personnel, and rescue provisions.

## Chapter 5 — Emergency Response, Reporting, and Closing

### 16. Emergency Action Plan

Know the site alarms, emergency contacts, evacuation route, and muster point. Keep emergency routes clear. During an emergency, remain calm and walk—do not run. Shut down equipment only when safe, report to the muster point, and remain until accounted for and released. Notify emergency authorities, then PAL supervision and EH&S.

### 17. Report Injuries Immediately

Report every injury, illness, exposure, property-damage incident, environmental release, and near miss to your foreman or supervisor immediately, no matter how small. Do not wait until tomorrow, next week, or until the condition worsens.

Immediate reporting allows PAL to provide care, document the event, correct hazards, and prevent another injury. Delayed reporting can prevent the incident from being handled correctly.

If removed from work or given restrictions, provide the required medical documents. Before returning, submit the appropriate doctor’s note or return-to-work release stating whether you may resume full duty or need approved restrictions. No employee will be penalized for refusing work they reasonably believe is unsafe. Stop and notify supervision.

### 18. Drugs, Alcohol, and Smoking

PAL maintains a drug-free and alcohol-free workplace. Alcohol during the workday, illegal drugs, controlled substances, paraphernalia, and reporting impaired are prohibited. Smoking is not permitted on New York City construction sites. Employees and applicants must follow PAL’s testing policy.

### 19. Closing

Safety depends on planning, training, communication, and personal responsibility. Participate in the daily safety meeting and Stretch and Flex. Keep training current. Wear required PPE. Follow Ladders Last. Use approved fall protection and barricades. Control electrical, fire, dust, and material-handling hazards. Keep the work area clean. Operate equipment only when authorized. Report every incident immediately.

When you are not trained, authorized, equipped, or certain, stop and ask. Thank you for completing PAL Environmental Services orientation.

### Checkpoint 5

When must an injury, exposure, incident, environmental release, or near miss be reported? Immediately, even when it appears minor.
